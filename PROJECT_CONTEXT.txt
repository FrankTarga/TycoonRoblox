# PROJECT CONTEXT — DINOSAUR FOSSIL TYCOON

**Engine:** Roblox Studio  
**Programming Language:** Luau  
**Development Methodology:** Test-Driven Development (TDD) — strict spec-first development  
**Project Type:** Tycoon / Simulation / Collection  
**Document Purpose:** Persistent development context for AI-assisted implementation.

---

## 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

Dinosaur Fossil Tycoon is a Roblox tycoon game focused on excavating fossils, extracting dinosaur DNA, incubating dinosaurs, and managing a dinosaur park.

Each player owns a personal plot where they can build and expand their dinosaur facility.

The main gameplay loop is:

1. Explore excavation zones.
2. Dig up fossils.
3. Clean fossils using brushes.
4. Extract DNA using a DNA Extractor machine.
5. Incubate DNA using a Dinosaur Incubator.
6. Obtain dinosaurs.
7. Place dinosaurs inside cages.
8. Generate passive income.
9. Upgrade equipment and unlock better excavation zones.
10. Expand the park and collect rarer dinosaurs.

The experience should emphasize physical interaction with the game world, progression, collection, and tycoon management.

---

## 2. MOST IMPORTANT GAMEPLAY RULE

**A single fossil part is enough to identify a dinosaur and generate its corresponding DNA.**

The player does NOT need to collect a complete skeleton or multiple fossil pieces.

Examples:

- T-Rex Tooth → T-Rex DNA
- Velociraptor Claw → Velociraptor DNA
- Triceratops Horn → Triceratops DNA
- Stegosaurus Plate → Stegosaurus DNA
- Spinosaurus Vertebra → Spinosaurus DNA

Different fossil parts may correspond to the same DinosaurID.

Do not implement a skeleton completion system, fossil set collection requirement, or multi-part assembly mechanic unless explicitly requested.

---

## 2.1 DEVELOPMENT METHODOLOGY — TEST-DRIVEN DEVELOPMENT (TDD)

**This project strictly follows Test-Driven Development (TDD) across all phases.**

Rules of TDD in this codebase:
1. **Spec-first development:** Before implementing any new service or feature, author the `.spec.lua` unit test file and CLI simulation tests defining all expected behaviors, validations, error conditions, and state transitions.
2. **Authoritative verification:** Run tests before and after code changes. Never consider a phase complete without 100% passing tests (`tests/run_tests.py` and `TestRunner.server.lua`).
3. **No untested code or claims:** Every service logic must be covered by automated tests. All commits must include their respective test suites.

## 3. CORE GAMEPLAY FLOW

The following sequence is mandatory:

### Step 1 — Excavation
- The player visits an unlocked excavation zone.
- The player uses a shovel to excavate a fossil.
- The fossil is added to the player's inventory.

### Step 2 — Fossil Cleaning
- The player selects a fossil from the inventory.
- The player physically carries it to the cleaning station.
- The player uses a brush to clean the fossil.
- Cleaning may reveal information about the fossil and improve its presentation or quality.

### Step 3 — DNA Extraction
- The player carries the cleaned fossil to the DNA Extractor.
- The player physically places the fossil inside the machine.
- The fossil is removed from the inventory.
- The fossil appears inside the machine.
- The extraction process begins.
- When processing finishes, DNA appears physically at the machine's output.
- DNA does NOT automatically enter the inventory.
- The player must manually collect the DNA.

### Step 4 — Dinosaur Incubation
- The player selects DNA from the inventory.
- The player physically carries it to the Dinosaur Incubator.
- The player places the DNA inside the machine.
- DNA is removed from the inventory.
- The DNA appears inside the incubator.
- The incubation process begins.
- When incubation finishes, a dinosaur is generated.
- The dinosaur is added to the player's inventory.
- The dinosaur is NOT automatically placed in a cage.

### Step 5 — Dinosaur Placement
- The player selects a dinosaur from the inventory.
- The player chooses an available cage in their own plot.
- The player physically places the dinosaur in the cage.
- The dinosaur appears inside the cage.
- The dinosaur starts generating passive income.

### Step 6 — Progression
- The player collects money.
- The player upgrades equipment.
- The player unlocks new excavation zones.
- The player finds rarer fossils and dinosaurs.
- The player expands their plot.

- Stored in the player's inventory.
- Must be cleaned before extraction.

- Stored in the player's inventory.
- Used by the Dinosaur Incubator.

### Dinosaurs
- Generated by the Dinosaur Incubator.
- Added to the player's inventory.
- Must be manually placed inside a cage.

Every item should have a unique identifier and relevant metadata.

Suggested item properties:

- Quality

## 8. DNA EXTRACTOR

The DNA Extractor is a physical machine inside the player's plot.

Required behavior:

1. Detect player interaction.
2. Validate the selected fossil.
3. Validate that the fossil belongs to the player.
4. Validate that the fossil is cleaned.
5. Remove the fossil from inventory.
6. Display the fossil inside the machine.
7. Start the extraction timer.
8. Generate DNA when processing finishes.
9. Display DNA physically at the output.
10. Allow the player to collect the DNA manually.

Machine states should include:

- Idle
- Processing
- OutputReady

The machine must not automatically transfer DNA to the player's inventory.

- Idle
- Incubating

1. **Spec-first development:** Before implementing service logic, write the test specification (`.spec.lua`) and CLI test cases defining all edge cases, validations, state transitions, and business rules.

3. **No untested assertions:** Never claim a system is tested without running automated tests.

1. Inspect the existing Explorer hierarchy.
2. Inspect relevant scripts and modules.
3. Understand existing dependencies.
4. Identify which files need to be created or modified.
5. Explain the implementation plan briefly.
6. Avoid rewriting working systems without necessity.

### During implementation:

1. Implement only the requested phase.
2. Reuse existing systems whenever possible.
3. Keep scripts modular.
4. Use clear naming conventions.
5. Validate server/client responsibilities.
6. Avoid unnecessary complexity.
7. Do not create duplicate systems.
8. Preserve previously working functionality.

### After implementation:

1. Review every modified script.
2. Check dependencies and references.
3. Look for syntax errors.
4. Look for runtime errors.
5. Check RemoteEvent validation.
6. Check item ownership and duplication risks.
7. Verify the expected gameplay flow.
8. Explain what was created or modified.
9. Explain how to test the implementation in Roblox Studio.
10. List known limitations or unfinished functionality.

Do not claim that a feature has been tested in-game unless an actual test was performed.
